# Course content rules

Use supplied DLSU course files as the source. Do not invent syllabus coverage, software requirements, practical proficiency or CIVINCO topic mappings. Keep references to the file and section used for each mapping.

## Lecture: Skills for the future

For each useful topic, identify a specific future course where it returns. Use wording such as: “This topic will be used and recalled in [course code — title].” Include a short reason for the connection. A prerequisite relationship alone does not prove that a particular topic is covered; check the course files.

Once all CIVINCO files arrive, review previous course nodes and backtrack the topics that appear in those files. Add the playful reminder: “This will also come back in CIVINCO, beware!” Only apply it to supported topic mappings, with a compact explanation of the connection.

## Laboratory: Skills for the future

Show concrete technical practice, verified software/tool skills, and a small piece of evidence the student could keep (for example, a report or project). Offer concise resume wording tied to that evidence. Academic completion or an app quiz does not by itself certify practical proficiency; do not automatically award a resume skill.

## Topic rundown

Keep the initial view compact: aim for five to seven grouped topics, one short line each. Put optional detail behind an expansion. Avoid long reading walls, duplicated syllabus text or a demand to finish the whole course in one session.

## Optional practice ideas

Minigames should practice one concept in a short session, with feedback explaining mistakes. Optional game rewards are cosmetic. They never record university completion or unlock CIVINCO/mastery by themselves. Use actual course material before authoring questions or scenarios.
