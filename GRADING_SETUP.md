# Syllabus-based grade calculators

Lectures use five tabs ending with Grade calculator (IV) and Mastery quiz (V). Labs use four tabs ending with their grade calculator. Students activate the course before accessing these tabs; Admin view opens every course tab without prerequisites.

There are 19 verified schemes in src/syllabus-data.mjs, including internal file/version/page references. Weights and passing thresholds are unchanged. Courses without a supplied grading scheme remain placeholders.

Students add individual earned/perfect quiz entries. At the owner's choice, each quiz percentage contributes equally to its category average; total points are not pooled. Long quizzes/exams use LQ labels, short quizzes use SQ, and knowledge checks use KCQ. Incomplete rows leave the category unknown. Rows may be removed or corrected.

Final Exam uses a single earned/perfect pair and displays its percentage automatically. Other requirements take the student's already computed average out of 100. Actual quiz and final-exam scores may exceed their perfect scores: 105/100 contributes 105%, without capping the awarded bonus. A required score above the perfect score is labelled as dependent on bonus points actually being awarded.

The calculator uses weighted percentages and the BASPHYS/ENGPHYS rule assigning 50% to the higher exam percentage and 35% to the other. Physics forecasts solve the changing-weight rule. Assessment maxima come from the student; 100 is an editable final-exam default, not a claim about the exam's maximum. Required-score increments are planning choices, not official rounding policies.

Attendance/submission conditions remain self-reported Met / Not met / Not checked requirements. Unmet conditions can override a numeric passing score. Enter instructor-adjusted scores after peer evaluations and penalties. No bonus award, official rounding rule, transmutation or attendance exception is invented.

Blank scores are unknown, not zero. Exact forecasts require other components' scores. Incomplete score cards show weighted percentage points earned toward the final grade; they never compare a renormalized average with a complete total. Physics leaves this subtotal unknown until the exam comparison can be made.

The 19 course-specific final-grade tables are verified in src/grade-scale.mjs, with internal syllabus/page references. Math uses thresholds 55/62/69/75/82/88/94, CE and labs 60/65/70/76/82/88/94, physics 50/58/66/73/80/87/94, BASCHEM 60/66/72/78/84/90/95, and ENGCHEM 60/66/72/78/84/90/96. Target-grade choices forecast the selected threshold while the actual outcome always uses the course's passing threshold. Unrounded totals use lower bounds; no official rounding policy is inferred from printed ranges.

Drafts do not change academic progress. Confirm and save records a complete self-reported subject-and-term grade, including the scores, numeric total, 0.0–4.0 grade, requirement checks and a snapshot of the grading scheme and source version. Reopening or correcting an earlier term uses that attempt's saved rules, so a later syllabus update cannot silently change its grade. A numeric pass with an unmet additional passing condition records 0.0; unchecked conditions cannot be confirmed. A confirmed pass marks the course completed; a confirmed failure marks it failed, preserving activation-based soft eligibility but blocking hard requirements. A confirmed passing hard prerequisite must precede the new enrollment term. Grades never award mastery or CIVINCO clearance.

Saved attempts live in academicRecords[courseId].gradeAttempts within the existing owner-protected student_progress JSON and cloud revision workflow. No database migration or new secret is required. Guest attempts stay on that browser; account attempts sync with that account; Admin view stays in its isolated device workspace. Drafts are separate by subject, term and view, and clear on account changes. Saved grades reopen read-only; Edit permits a confirmed correction and preserves the earlier snapshot in revisions.

Activate retake starts a fresh attempt in a later journey term and advances the personal journey term. The earlier failed grade remains available. Extended journey terms use the existing Year IV standing bucket while displaying their actual year/term labels; storage supports up to 60 journey terms. Unstarted hard-descendant plans inherit the maximum delay, rather than adding the same delay repeatedly. Co-requisites remain alongside or after their lectures. Started and completed records keep their taken terms. The map keeps previous failed attempt nodes, adds the current/planned retake, and supports additional term charts and bounded mobile rendering. These are personal plans: actual enrollment, course offerings and load limits are not inferred.
